# Question-Driven Arc Design

Use this model when an arc is organized around a dramatic question. It is one
option, not the definition of an arc. For episodic, contemplative, or associative
work, use the connections and contributions the author wants instead.

Possible movements include an invitation or hook, complications, a reframing,
a difficult choice, and consequences. Use only those that help. A midpoint
reversal is not compulsory; discovery, repetition, and gradual accumulation
can sustain attention. Character successes need not automatically reduce interest.

Follow the question through the intended reader experience. Give consequential
moments enough room and allow recovery or ordinary life where they contribute.
Place shifts where the material earns them rather than at a prescribed fraction.
Resolution may answer, complicate, or deliberately leave the question open.
An arc can offer a complete experience without launching the next crisis.
