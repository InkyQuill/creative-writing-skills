# Light-Novel Profile in English

Realize the light-novel profile in idiomatic English. Preserve quick access to
viewpoint and scene pressure without imitating Japanese word order, honorific
exposition, or translation punctuation unless the project's voice explicitly
uses them.

## Cadence

Let paragraph breaks mark turns of attention, reaction, and comic timing.
Mix concise lines with fuller sentences so speed has contrast. A short line
lands because the surrounding prose gives it room; a page of fragments loses
that effect. Keep references clear during fast exchanges, but do not repeat
names merely to mimic translated dialogue.

Example: "Mara had rehearsed three speeches. Naturally, the dragon chose the
fourth option." The turn is compact, viewpoint-colored, and legible without
explaining the joke.

## Dialogue and Interior Voice

Use contractions, interruption, understatement, and register shifts native to
the chosen English variety. Dialogue may carry momentum, but each speaker still
needs distinct priorities and evasions. Interior reactions can be immediate
and witty without becoming a running caption for every visible action.

## Allowed Variation and Failure Modes

Allow dense description, formal characters, and slow emotional aftermath when
the scene earns them. Do not flatten vocabulary into "easy English," turn every
beat into banter, or append an explanatory reaction after the reader already
understood. Translation-shaped repetition is a failure unless documented as
the author's chosen voice.
