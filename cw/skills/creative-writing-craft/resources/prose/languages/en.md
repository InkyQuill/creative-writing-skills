# English Prose Defaults

Write and review English prose as English, not as a neutral template for every
language. Let the project's documented variety—British, American, Canadian,
or another valid convention—settle spelling and punctuation choices.

## Sentence and Paragraph Movement

Prefer a clear relationship between clauses. English usually makes actors and
actions legible early, but inversion, fragments, periodic sentences, and long
subordination remain available when voice or suspense earns them. Do not force
variety by rotating sentence openers mechanically. Repeated pronoun or article
openings are a signal only when they produce audible monotony in context.

Use punctuation according to syntax and the chosen house style. Document such
choices as the serial comma, quotation punctuation, and em-dash spacing rather
than treating one variant as universal. Preserve purposeful interruption and
parenthesis; fix a mark when it obscures the clause relationship.

## Dialogue and Diction

Follow the project's quotation convention. In conventional English dialogue,
quotation marks contain speech: "I didn't promise that." Tags, action beats,
and omissions should serve voice and clarity. Plain tags such as *said* can
recede, but they are not automatically superior; repeated or conspicuous tags
are judged by the passage's rhythm and the speaker's voice.

Prefer exact words over inflated substitutes, while allowing a character's
register to be blunt, ornate, technical, dialectal, or unstable. A repeated
ordinary word may be invisible; a repeated distinctive word may ring. Judge
the actual reader effect rather than applying an English vocabulary blacklist.

## Allowed Variation and Failure Modes

Contractions, dialect spelling, run-ons, fragments, and unusual punctuation
may be intentional voice. Require project evidence before normalizing them.
Common failures are translation-shaped syntax, uniform subject-first cadence,
and polishing every voice into the same fluent standard English. Correct
grammar where the text claims standard usage; protect documented deviation.
