# Investigating Structural Problems

First establish a concrete mismatch between the passage and the intended
experience. A framework's missing beat is not evidence of failure.

- **A middle that loses purpose:** examine whether repetition has stopped
  adding interest, whether a question is exhausted, or whether a quieter
  movement needs room. A reversal is one option, not the automatic remedy.
- **A rushed ending:** identify what the reader needs time to experience or
  understand; extend, simplify, or preserve deliberate abruptness as appropriate.
- **An apparently detachable arc:** ask what atmosphere, companionship, theme,
  or experience would disappear with it before calling it filler.
- **Disconnected scenes:** check the intended connection—causal, associative,
  tonal, thematic, or cumulative—before imposing a causal chain.
- **Unchanging stakes:** assess whether stability is rewarding or unwanted.
  Do not raise the danger merely to satisfy an escalation rule.

Use diagrams for complex chronology or relationships when they clarify the
actual problem, not as a required planning artifact.
