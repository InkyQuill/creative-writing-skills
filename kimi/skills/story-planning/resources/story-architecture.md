# Story Architecture

Choose structural questions that fit the work. Causal plots, episodic journeys,
portraits, contemplative narratives, comic cycles, and associative structures
need not move in the same way. Do not impose escalation, transformation, or
closure merely because a framework supplies those stages.

## Work at the Relevant Scale

A series, arc, chapter, scene, or passage can earn its place by changing events,
deepening perception, creating atmosphere, offering companionship, sustaining
comedy, or letting the reader inhabit a moment. Ask what the work would lose
without it. Lack of plot advancement is not sufficient grounds for a cut.

For causal plots, trace consequences and distinguish meaningful coincidence
from convenience. For episodic or associative work, examine thematic,
perceptual, tonal, or cumulative connections. Reorderability alone is not a
failure. Let stakes rise, recede, recur, or shift when that fits the experience.

Track genuine promises rather than turning every detail into a promised payoff.
An unresolved question can be deliberate; a surprise needs enough grounding
for the kind of reading the author wants. Clarify expectations without forcing
closure or explaining away ambiguity.
