# Review rubric

## Fidelity

Compare each selected output unit with its primary source: who acts, what is
asserted or withheld, tense/aspect, relationships, negation, causality and imagery.
Check missing or repeated material across unit boundaries. Distinguish a
legitimate split/merge of paragraphs from a semantic omission. Preserve ambiguity
and unreliable narration; later knowledge must not leak into earlier wording.

Use auxiliary editions to investigate alternatives, not to overrule the primary
silently. Example: if an English translation changes a question into an
assertion, inspect Japanese wording and context before making the same change
in Russian. Record the discrepancy with both locators.

## Literary reading

Read the target passage continuously. Does each voice retain its intended
register, emotional restraint, social relation and rhythm? Does syntax sound
natural while preserving the source's purposeful difficulty or strangeness?
Check name forms, forms of address, wordplay, repeated motifs and transition
joins against accepted target decisions. A calm threat need not become slang or
an exclamation to read forcefully.

## Findings

For each finding record severity (`error`, `decision-conflict`, `suggestion`),
source edition/volume/unit and chapter/scene, target direction/file/passage,
evidence, diagnosis, and a proposed correction or unresolved choice. Put a
possible term/voice revision into proposed memory rather than silently changing
the accepted rule.

After revision inspect affected passages and joins again. Report unchecked
coverage and unavailable evidence. A technical pass establishes file/revision
consistency only; a self-review is not an independent translator's evaluation.

For a packet that used external memory, obtain current public observations and
pass their strict references through `--external-memory-observed` during status
and acceptance preparation. Preserve the captured provider, namespace, record
kind, record ID, source revision, and direction scope in the finding. The known
changed or missing references require `needs-review` and take precedence over an
unverified marker or fallback note. Unavailable observation or a captured marker
remains `unknown`. A historical unknown recorded at acceptance remains historical;
later matching observations may establish current freshness for strict references
without rewriting it. External checking never relaxes local source, prose,
review, accepted-base, coverage, or path checks.
