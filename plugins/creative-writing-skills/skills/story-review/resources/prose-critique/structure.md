# Structure Review

Judge structure against the author's form and intended experience. The lenses
in `$story-planning`'s `resources/story-architecture.md` are optional methods,
not a universal checklist for fiction.

## Connection and Contribution

Ask what connects passages: causation, place, character, theme, contrast,
association, ritual, or accumulation. In a causal plot, flag missing causes
when they undermine credibility. In episodic work, reordering may be possible
without being a defect. Do not require each event to drive the next.

Ask what the book would lose if a passage were removed. Atmosphere, information,
quiet companionship, travel, reflection, and comic repetition can be valuable
experiences. Propose a cut only when its benefit exceeds that loss. A scene
need not change a relationship or raise stakes to earn its place.

## Rhythm and Stakes

Locate an unwanted effect in the actual text: a development rushed beyond
comprehension, an emotional moment given too little room, or repetition that
ceases to contribute. Slow reading and sustained quiet can be intentional.
Do not diagnose pacing from length, low conflict, or a model's imagined boredom
alone. State your reading as an interpretation with evidence.

Where suspense is intended, assess what may be lost and why that matters.
Stakes can deepen, diminish, shift, or remain modest; continuous escalation is
not a requirement. Investigate whether repetition or predictability is unwanted
before changing a comforting or comic pattern.

## Expectation and Payoff

Distinguish an established promise from an incidental detail, a character's
expectation, or a critic's preference. Trace relevant setup and consequences.
Flag an abandoned promise when the available text supports that conclusion;
otherwise record the scope limit. Preserve deliberate openness and ambiguity.

Return supported problems, strengths to preserve, optional alternatives, and
material uncertainty. No substantial structural change is a valid finding.
