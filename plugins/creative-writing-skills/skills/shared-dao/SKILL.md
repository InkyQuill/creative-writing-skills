# Story Vocabulary

Distinguish reference identity from expressions used in the story. Resolve
accidental confusion while preserving meaningful ambiguity, polysemy, aliases,
misunderstandings, and differences between cultures, periods, and speakers.

Use `$project-bootstrap` when instruction discovery is needed.
Before adding a term, read the relevant author notes and actual prose usage.
Use the existing vocabulary location; no folder structure or glossary is
required for a short task. `$kb-management` owns durable record updates and
`$story-memory` owns context selection and knowledge boundaries.

## Record Meaning in Context

For a recurring term, record only the fields needed to prevent mistakes:

- referent or concept, with a stable reference label where useful;
- expression and its language, speaker/group, period, and register;
- aliases or competing meanings, and whether the relationship is established;
- source and status: author-stated, accepted evidence, or provisional;
- what the reader and each speaker can know at the relevant story time.

For example, priests may call an event a blessing, physicians a condition,
and soldiers a curse. Record the relationship if established; do not replace
all three with a supposedly clearer word. If their identity is a future reveal,
preserve that knowledge boundary in both prose and handoffs.

## Resolve Genuine Conflicts

When two records accidentally name the same thing differently, determine
whether this is drift or intentional variation. When one name has several
meanings, document scope rather than automatically renaming a concept.
Ask only when the unresolved distinction materially affects the requested work.
Never use glossary consistency as permission to rewrite character language.

Suggest a clearer reference label when it would help the author, but do not
rename established story terms merely because another label seems cleaner.
Keep provisional possibilities open. A metaphor, disputed belief, slur, or
in-world misnomer can be correct characterization without being world truth.
